# Sweep 2 instructions (floridaspending.com)

You are running one lane of a one-time sweep that screens Florida local-government agenda items from the past 12 months and forward, and adds the ones that qualify to the review sheet. Several lanes run in parallel on separate item lists. The task prompt gives your LANE number and your progress ROW. Work quietly and finish with a two-line reply.

Sheet: Claude Sheet https://claude.ai/artifact/Ux7za8x7Mhgn7xfnBoh34f (project id e258f5e4-8500-45e1-a178-91259d3dc9bc). Use the Claude Docs connector tools (load with ToolSearch if needed) and call guide with items ["topic.sheets"] before the first Sheet call. Never change or clear existing cells other than your progress cells, and never delete rows.

1. Read sheet "Backlog progress", row ROW: B = last item number processed in this lane (n), C = total. If B >= C, do nothing else and reply exactly: "Sweep 2 lane LANE complete; this scheduled task can be turned off."
2. curl -sL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/AskAIJeeves/fl-agenda-watch/main/data/sweep2/lane_LANE.json -o lane.json. With python, take the next 60 items with n greater than B, in order. Fields: id, government, body, meeting_date, stage (upcoming / early_warning = vote not yet held; past_meeting), days_until_meeting, categories, amount (largest dollar figure in the text; may be a whole budget or project-wide figure), flow (in / out / mixed guess), passed (clerk's result, may be null), recipient, link, project_id, project_total, funding_source, paying_fund, paying_fund_expenditures, pct_of_paying_fund, possible_duplicate_label, text (agenda title or PDF passage, possibly messy).
3. Read the "Queue" and "Backlog" sheets: header row (locate columns by header name, never by position), every Item ID, and Government, Description, Amount, Meeting date of existing rows, plus Queue rows whose Stage is "statement" (governments with stated shortfalls or cuts).
4. For each item, decide whether it is a real spending decision (not minutes chatter, an incoming grant, a grant application, a budget table, boilerplate, a check register, an investment report, or a budget adoption). If not, skip it. Check the direction of the money; receipts rarely qualify. If the text shows a different approved amount than the amount field, use that and recompute its percent of paying_fund_expenditures. Confirm the funding source from the text.
   Decide whether the decision is still PENDING: the vote has not happened (stage upcoming or early_warning), or the text shows it still needs a later or final approval (a first reading of an ordinance, a committee or CRA board recommendation that goes to the full commission or council, a staff recommendation for a later meeting, a resolution of intent). An item is NOT pending if the later approval's date has already passed (for example a budget hearing in September) or the committee meeting was more than 60 days ago; treat those as past items with "Outcome not verified".
   Score 1 to 5 each: (a) nonessential vs core service (roads, water, public safety are core), (b) vanity or novelty, (c) benefits officials' image, comfort or travel, or specific private businesses or property, (d) competition bypassed, (e) clashes with a claimed budget shortfall; total = a + 2b + 2c + d + e.
   KEEP the item if any of:
   - PENDING and score 12 or more;
   - PENDING and the approved amount is $1,000,000 or more, or 0.5% or more of paying_fund_expenditures;
   - not pending and score 15 or more;
   - not pending and the approved amount is $5,000,000 or more, or 2% or more of paying_fund_expenditures.
   Large items kept only for their size are listed plainly: no red-flag language, category includes "large_spending". Skip routine items regardless of size: incoming grants, budget clean-ups, conduit bonds, refunding bonds, payroll, utility repairs, insurance renewals, millage levies, report backup pages, contract boilerplate. Grants or payments to private individuals: name the recipient exactly as the government's record does; naming a person is not a reason to flag. Public money granted directly to an individual is private benefit (score (c) at 5, category private_benefit) and is Feature-worthy.
   Apply the rubric identically regardless of the recipient's cause, viewpoint or politics; name recipients exactly as they name themselves.
   ALSO SKIP (the reviewer's rules):
   - Iffy items: the text does not make clear what is being approved, to whom, or for what. Skip rather than guess.
   - Items with no meeting date and nothing in the text placing them within the past 12 months or ahead.
   - Items with no dollar amount, unless the description itself shows spending people would want to know about: private benefit, frivolous or vanity spending, perks for officials, or competition waived. A plain contract or grant with no amount is skipped.
5. Duplicates: skip any item whose id is already an Item ID in either sheet, and any item that is confidently the same approval as an existing row (same government and recipient or address, and the same amount or item). If it might be the same but you cannot tell, keep it and add "May repeat an existing entry (date)." to its Description.
6. Suggested (Feature or Keep): Feature if at least two of: (1) PENDING; (2) self-explanatory in one line to a stranger; (3) same government claims a shortfall or is cutting services; (4) competition waived, emergency ratified after the fact, or private benefit; (5) 1% or more of paying_fund_expenditures (if null, judge by the government's size). Otherwise Keep. Core-service items with no red flag are Keep. Needs your review: only when a reasonable person could disagree with publishing it as described (record ambiguous about what is approved, the amount or the direction; a sensitive matter such as a settlement with a person, a medical or personnel matter, a victim or a minor; or the description could be read as an accusation). Expect very few.
7. Where rows go: PENDING items and any item flagged for review go to the "Queue" sheet; all others go to the "Backlog" sheet. Queue has "Digest section" and "Needs your review" where Backlog has "Priority group". Values under matching headers: Decision (null), Suggested, Note (null), Government, Description (plain factual line under 300 characters: body, what is approved, recipient as named, amount, and its share of the paying fund when known, written as "(X% of general fund)" or "(X% of [government]'s [enterprise / special revenue / capital projects / internal service] funds)"; for a pending item say what approval is still ahead, e.g. "Final vote pending (second reading)"; for past meetings add "Result: Pass/Fail per clerk" if passed is set, else "Outcome not verified"; "Direction unclear" if unclear), Shortfall context (that government's stated shortfall or cut with its figure, else null), Amount (number; negative when received; null if none), Link, Stage (upcoming, early_warning, past_meeting, or "pending_final" when the meeting has passed but a final approval is still ahead), Meeting date (YYYY-MM-DD), Category (categories joined with "; "; include private_benefit where it applies, large_spending when kept for size), Score, Digest section ("Sweep 2") or Priority group ("sweep2"), Added (today, YYYY-MM-DD), Item ID, Project ID, Tracked project total (number or null), Possible duplicate (null), Needs your review (Queue only).
8. Writing: readSheets for each sheet's filledRange, then one applyEdits per sheet with base = the revision you read, writing new rows starting at the first empty row under the table. Many lanes write to the same sheets: if an update is refused for its base, read filledRange and the revision again and retry, up to five times. Then write the new last n processed to "Backlog progress" column B of row ROW and today's date to column D of that row, in a separate applyEdits (retry the same way).
9. Never invent facts: describe only what the item text and link support. No em-dashes. Never use the words cronyism, favoritism or corruption.
10. Reply with two lines: n range processed, rows added to Queue and Backlog (how many pending, how many kept for size), items flagged for review; and anything that failed.
